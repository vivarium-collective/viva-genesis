Claim

A protocell's chemistry can change its physical fate. An internal autocatalytic/metabolic source makes an osmolyte; its accumulation raises the internal osmotic pressure (van 't Hoff), that pressure draws solvent across the semi-permeable membrane, the compartment expands, and at a size threshold it divides — a physical selection mechanism with no abstract fitness score. Whether this produces division rather than lysis or starvation is set by the membrane permeability against the network throughput — there is a bounded viable band in which net growth leads to repeated division, flanked by starvation (too permeable — the product leaks before the osmotic pressure can build) and lysis (too impermeable — the pressure bursts the membrane). Mass is conserved to machine precision across growth and division.

Background

Question Does an internal autocatalytic/metabolic chemistry change a compartment's PHYSICAL fate — accumulating product, generating osmotic pressure, drawing in solvent, expanding, and forcing division — and under what permeability regime does it divide rather than lyse or starve?
Hypothesis Product accumulation from an internal autocatalytic source generates osmotic pressure that drives solvent influx and volume growth to a division threshold; there is a bounded membrane-permeability band in which the compartment sustains net growth leading to division, flanked by starvation (too permeable, the product leaks before osmotic pressure can build) and lysis (too impermeable, the pressure bursts the membrane).

Model & approach

Couple a reduced autocatalytic osmolyte source to compartment osmotic physics (osmotic pressure → solvent influx → volume → division) with a semi-permeable membrane; map the (network throughput × membrane permeability) plane and locate the viable growth→division band between starvation and lysis, conserving mass across growth and division.

Result

The matched (favorable) compartment divides 8 times over the run (net growth to division, outcome = divide), with the mass-balance residual ~1e-32 mol (conserved across every division). The viable growth→division band is ~25% of the (throughput × permeability) grid, bounded by starvation (too permeable → no division, net growth ~1.00) and lysis (too impermeable → membrane bursts at ~1.5e5 Pa). Zero-throughput ablation gives no division and essentially no growth.
