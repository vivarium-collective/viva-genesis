Chemical Inheritance: Can a Chemical Difference Be Transmitted?

Q Can a protocell transmit a heritable chemical difference to its daughters at a feasible cost, without presuming a long high-fidelity RNA template?

Claim

A protocell transmits a heritable chemical difference to its daughters at a feasible cost, and heredity need not presume a long high-fidelity RNA template: competing modules graded head-to-head show inheritance beats a non-heritable control and compositional/hybrid heredity escapes the Eigen length catastrophe (og-06), and an information-per-cost frontier ranks six mechanisms by heritable bits per joule with attachment conditional on budget (og-06-alt). Supported. Selectability is tested separately in og-evolvability.

Background

Question Can a protocell transmit a heritable chemical difference to its daughters at a feasible cost, without presuming a long high-fidelity RNA template?
Hypothesis Compositional and hybrid inheritance modules transmit functional information above an error threshold at lower cost than a long RNA template, so heredity can be chemical rather than genetic from the start.

Model & approach

Grade competing inheritance modules head-to-head above an error threshold (og-06) and map an information-per-cost frontier over six mechanisms under a closing information ledger (og-06-alt); each arm keeps its own verdict, matched null, findings and run provenance.
