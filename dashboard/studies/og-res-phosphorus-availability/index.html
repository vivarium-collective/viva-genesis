The Phosphorus Bottleneck

Q Can the same thermal-gradient vent pore that concentrates carbon and nitrogen also make phosphorus chemically available, or does phosphate precipitate and adsorb before it is useful?

Overview

Claim

A P-speciation network coupled to the thermal pore makes reactive phosphorus net-positive only in a bounded phosphate-source by pore-enrichment window where condensation and activation outrun the precipitation and adsorption sink; at the favourable point reactive P accumulates to about 94% of the supplied phosphorus with only about 6% precipitated. The same enrichment that concentrates carbon co-concentrates phosphorus, whereas the open-pore ablation precipitates about 96% of it before it is useful. In the model phosphorus is thus a localization-gated bottleneck rather than a freely available input.

Background

Question Can the same thermal-gradient vent pore that concentrates carbon and nitrogen also make phosphorus chemically available, or does phosphate precipitate and adsorb before it is useful?
Hypothesis In a thermal-gradient pore (coupled from og-03), enrichment drives phosphate condensation and activation steeply enough that reactive-P formation outruns the first-order precipitation/adsorption sink in a bounded (source × enrichment) region — and that same enrichment co-concentrates P alongside carbon; without the gradient (open pore), precipitation wins and no reactive P accumulates.

Model & approach

Build a real P-speciation process (phosphate <-> polyphosphate <-> reactive-P with a precipitation/adsorption mineral sink), couple it to the og-03 thermal pore, and map where reactive-P availability beats loss and co-concentrates with carbon.

A bounded local window exists where reactive P is produced faster than it is lost.

Key assumptions

  • Reactive-P availability arises from the balance of condensation, activation, hydrolysis and precipitation/adsorption — not assumed; the precipitation sink is explicit.

Limitations

What this study explicitly does NOT validate (prevents overclaim).

  • The P-speciation network is a lumped reduced model (single reactive-P pool, reversible mineral sink); rate constants are calibrated to the precipitation-vs-reactive competition, not fit to a mineral assemblage.
  • The co-concentration argument uses the og-03 trap enrichment as a species-agnostic multiplier into the P network; a fully coupled spatial C+P pore solve is deferred.
  • natcomm2023phosphorus / natcomm2025phosphateactivation (plan refs) are not yet on main's papers.bib — recorded as pending proposed_inputs, not cited here.
