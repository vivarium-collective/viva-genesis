Carbon Fixation: Does H2 Reduce CO2 at the Vent?

Q Does H2-driven CO2 reduction over Fe/Ni/S catalysts fix carbon and reproduce lab yield benchmarks at the vent — and can the model classes agree on which limitation stops it?

Claim

H2-driven CO2 reduction fixes carbon at the vent. Over Fe/Ni/S catalysts the model reproduces the Preiner-2020 yield hierarchy (formate ~200 mM >> acetate ~100 uM >> pyruvate ~10 uM) and sustains net organic-carbon flux in a bounded pH-gradient x dissolved-H2 region; formate collapses to <1 mM in every no-gradient / no-H2 / no-catalyst control, and carbon fixation spends the proton gradient (~2 H+/carbon, chemiosmotic backpressure) (og-01, canonical, supported). Under explicit electrochemistry the vent cell is activation (catalyst) limited where the wall conducts and its current sits in the Altair-2025 window, but the two model classes agree on the dominant-limitation label on only 47% of the favorable region (below the pre-registered 80% gate), so the limitation attribution is structure-sensitive and og-01-cal-electrochemistry is refuted on that agreement question (og-01-cal, refuted; og-01-cal-dx planned to diagnose). Consolidated headline: supported — carbon fixation works and reproduces lab benchmarks; the mechanistic attribution of its rate-limiting step is not yet model-class-robust. Every matched null and arm verdict kept, including the refutation.
